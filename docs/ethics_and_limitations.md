## 1. Executive Mandate & Responsible AI Principles

### 1.1 The Anti-Harm Mandate
The AI-Based Deepfake Image Detection System is engineered exclusively as a **diagnostic decision-support tool** for digital identity and forensics analysts. It is **explicitly prohibited** from being deployed as an autonomous, self-executing gatekeeper to:
- Unilaterally deny an individual access to financial services, bank accounts, or credit.
- Automatically reject government identity verification, citizenship applications, or border crossings.
- Act as the sole piece of evidence in civil fraud litigation or criminal prosecutions.
- Unilaterally ban social media or communications accounts without certified human review.

### 1.2 Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Requirement
Every output generated by this system represents a **statistical consistency score**, not a definitive judgment of fact. The workflow requires that any image flagged with high synthetic probability ($\ge 0.65$) or marked as ambiguous/inconclusive ($0.35 < \text{score} < 0.65$) must be routed to a qualified forensic or risk analyst for secondary review.

## 2. Forensic Integrity & Legal Chain-of-Custody

### 2.1 The Non-Claims Doctrine (What the Model Does NOT Prove)
To prevent misrepresentation in academic defense, media coverage, or commercial evaluation, the following legal disclosures are binding:

1. **Still Images Do NOT Prove Liveness:** A portrait image verified as "Real / Consistent with Natural Capture" ($s \le 0.35$) proves only that the digital image contains sensor noise and structural features characteristic of natural camera captures. It does **not** prove physical human liveness, and does not detect presentation attacks (e.g., printed paper photographs, high-resolution tablet replays, or silicone masks held before a camera).
2. **Absence of Synthetic Traces Is NOT Proof of Authenticity:** Generative models improve continuously. A score of $0.10$ means the system detected no statistical artifacts matching its known generator distributions; it does not guarantee the image was not created by an advanced, unreleased generative model.
3. **Missing Metadata Is NOT Evidence of Manipulation:** Commercial messaging platforms (WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram, iMessage) and social networks (X, Instagram, Facebook) routinely strip all EXIF, GPS, and camera metadata during image compression and transit. An image lacking EXIF metadata is standard web consumer media, not fraud.
4. **Presence of Artifacts Is NOT Proof of Malicious Fraud:** Real photographs processed through digital portrait retouching, beauty filters, aggressive night-mode HDR, or lossy social media compression can exhibit high-frequency distortions that trigger false positive detections.

### 2.2 Forensic Chain-of-Custody Standards
When utilizing this system in forensic investigations, operators must preserve digital chain-of-custody:
- **Cryptographic Asset Hashing:** Ingested images must be hashed via SHA-256 immediately upon arrival before any in-memory transformations.
- **Audit Telemetry Persistence:** Forensic reports must record: SHA-256 hash, UTC timestamp, exact software version, model checkpoint hash, raw logit, calibrated probability, and preprocessing parameters.
- **Bit-Level Immutability:** The original uploaded binary stream must remain unaltered.

## 3. Demographic Fairness & Disparate Impact Analysis

### 3.1 Known Biases in Pretraining & Benchmark Datasets
- **Source Dataset Skew:** The primary benchmark dataset (GenImage) relies on ImageNet-1K for authentic training samples and text-to-image models (Stable Diffusion v1.4/v1.5) trained on subsets of LAION-5B. Both foundation corpuses exhibit documented demographic skews toward lighter skin tones (Fitzpatrick types I–III) and Western facial morphologies.
- **Sensor & Exposure Disparities:** Commercial smartphone cameras frequently apply unequal dynamic range and noise-reduction algorithms across different skin tones and hair textures. Under-exposed portraits of darker-skinned individuals (Fitzpatrick types V–VI) may suffer higher high-frequency sensor noise attenuation, potentially increasing false positive rates.

### 3.2 Mitigation & Monitoring Obligations
1. **Model Card Disclosure:** Disclose dataset demographic composition and known representation limitations in `reports/model_card.md`.
2. **Prohibition of Biometric Identification:** The system must never be coupled with face recognition embeddings to perform 1:1 or 1:N identity matching.
3. **Continuous Disparity Auditing:** When deploying beyond the academic prototype, evaluation must track False Positive Rates ($FPR$) across demographic cohorts to verify that no protected group experiences an $FPR$ more than $1.25\times$ the overall population average (Four-Fifths Rule / Disparate Impact threshold).

### Detailed Analysis of Failure Modes

1. **The Generalization Cliff:**
   - Detectors trained on Stable Diffusion v1.4/v1.5 learn specific mathematical traits of its AutoencoderKL latent decoder. When tested on newer models with completely different decoders (e.g., DALL-E 3 or Flux), detection accuracy can drop from $>90\%$ to near-chance ($55\%$).
   - *Mitigation:* Explicitly disclosed in PRD Section 1.1; evaluated directly on Synthbuster held-out benchmark.

2. **Aggressive Social Media Compression:**
   - When an image is shared across messaging apps, it is re-compressed with aggressive discrete cosine quantization. This eliminates the high-frequency residual traces that deep convolutional networks rely upon.
   - *Mitigation:* The system detects high-frequency attenuation via Laplacian blur variance and shifts the verdict into the **Abstention band** (`Inconclusive`) rather than emitting a false negative.

3. **Cosmetic Airbrushing & Commercial Studio Lighting:**
   - Professional fashion and corporate headshots frequently undergo digital skin smoothing, blemish removal, and HDR tone mapping. These modifications smooth natural skin pores, mimicking the plastic smoothness of early GAN generators.
   - *Mitigation:* The UI presents a permanent warning that digital cosmetic editing may elevate synthetic scores.

4. **Screen Recapture & Physical Presentation Attacks:**
   - When an attacker photographs an LCD or OLED screen displaying a real photo, the camera sensor interacts with the display subpixel grid, creating high-frequency Moiré fringes.
   - *Mitigation:* Cataloged as an out-of-scope Presentation Attack condition requiring dedicated physical liveness detection hardware.

5. **Adversarial Evasion:**
   - Deepfake detectors are differentiable neural networks vulnerable to adversarial gradient attacks (e.g., Projected Gradient Descent / PGD). An attacker with white-box access can add imperceptible perturbation noise ($\delta$) that shifts a synthetic image into the authentic classification band without visible degradation.
   - *Mitigation:* Production deployments must restrict API rate limits, mask raw gradient responses, and apply defensive input randomization.

## 5. Regulatory, Compliance & Standards Alignment

### 5.1 European Union Artificial Intelligence Act (EU AI Act)
Under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (EU AI Act), AI systems utilized in biometric categorization, identity verification, and access to essential private/public services are categorized as **High-Risk AI Systems**:

| EU AI Act Article | Regulatory Requirement | System Compliance Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Article 9** | Risk Management System | Continuous risk logging, failure mode taxonomy, and stress testing. |
| **Article 10** | Data Governance & Manifests | Manifest generation with cryptographic hashes and split de-leakage. |
| **Article 11** | Technical Documentation | Comprehensive documentation suite (`prd.md`, `architecture.md`, `model_card.md`). |
| **Article 12** | Record-Keeping & Logging | Structured JSON audit exports with timestamps, hashes, and model parameters. |
| **Article 13** | Transparency & Provision of Info | Mandatory on-screen forensic notices and score caveats on every prediction. |
| **Article 14** | Human Oversight | Three-tier decision engine with explicit abstention and triage routing. |
| **Article 15** | Accuracy, Robustness & Security | ECE calibration, locked evaluation, and decompression bomb defenses. |

### 5.2 NIST AI Risk Management Framework (NIST AI RMF 1.0)
The system aligns directly with the core NIST AI RMF functions:
- **GOVERN:** Clear boundaries establishing that the system is an academic research prototype and diagnostic triage tool.
- **MAP:** Categorization of input edge cases, compression artifacts, and demographic limitations.
- **MEASURE:** Full metric reporting (ROC-AUC, Macro-F1, EER, ECE) and generalization gap tracking on external benchmarks.
- **MANAGE:** Implementation of temperature scaling, three-tier abstention bands, and input sanitization barriers.

### 5.3 General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)
- **Article 22 Compliance:** By mandating human-in-the-loop review for all consequential actions, the system ensures data subjects are never subjected to purely automated decisions with legal or significant effects.
- **Data Minimization & Ephemeral Storage:** User uploads are held in memory during the interactive session turn and purged immediately upon completion, adhering to GDPR Article 5(1)(c).

### 5.4 Long-Term Complement: C2PA Cryptographic Provenance
The forensics community universally acknowledges that passive, probabilistic deepfake detection is inherently reactive: as generative models improve, the margin between natural and synthetic imagery approaches zero.
- **Strategic Direction:** In enterprise digital identity verification, passive classification must be coupled with active cryptographic asset provenance: the **Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity (C2PA)**.
- **C2PA Standard:** Embeds cryptographically signed hardware certificates at capture time (e.g., inside camera sensors or secure smartphone enclaves), proving camera make, location, timestamp, and edit history through tamper-evident public-key infrastructure.
